# ADR 0001: Local, versioned copy distribution

Status: accepted. Use a standard-library installer to copy a reviewed checkout's canonical docs, profiles and native Codex skills into a project. Store exact hashes and a content digest; leave project-owned instructions and configuration untouched. This works offline, avoids a central runtime and supports review and rollback in Git. Tradeoffs: copies need explicit updates; semantic rule conflicts require human review; interrupted multi-file updates need recovery. Do not use undocumented Codex orchestration or claim instructions enforce policy.

Expiry date: 2028-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07
Expiry and review handling: this record predates the workflow requirement that every decision record carry an explicit expiry, and it is brought under that rule here rather than exempted from it. Two gaps are recorded and deliberately not filled: the original states no owner and no approval date, and neither is reconstructed, because naming an approver or a date that was never written down would turn an acknowledged gap into a false audit record. Treat the owner as unrecorded until the foundation owner confirms it, and do not read "accepted" as evidence of who accepted it or when. The dates above are aligned with ADR 0002 and ADR 0003 so the three are reconciled in one pass.

Overdue handling differs from those two records because this one grants no permission. It chose a distribution mechanism, so there is nothing to suspend and a lapse cannot widen anything; the workflow's fallback rule has no permission to fall back from. An overdue review therefore means only that the choice is unexamined, which is reported under INS-7 alongside the instruction-set reconciliation and does not stop the installer or invalidate installed content. At expiry the mechanism stays in place and a superseding record is required to confirm or replace it, because reverting would leave no distribution method at all and is not a safe direction. Expiry here compels a decision rather than a fallback.
